A phasescope, a live spectrum and a retained waterfall over server audio buses. A phasescope reads a stereo pair and draws the 45°-rotated Lissajous figure — mono reads as a vertical line, anti-phase as horizontal, a wide field fills the lozenge — with a running correlation read-out beneath. A spectrum reads one bus and draws one forward FFT per frame on a log frequency axis; it is navigable, which over a spectrum means its frequency axis. And a waterfall is the spectrogram presentation over the same live bus with a retention span: a forward-only source has no addressable past, so retention supplies one and the time axis becomes navigable exactly like a spectrogram computed from a file. The source is a sine whose stereo image is swept from this page: at θ = 0 the two channels are identical (mono, r = +1), at 90° the right is a decorrelated detuned tone (wide, r ≈ 0), at 180° it is the left inverted (anti-phase, r = −1).
