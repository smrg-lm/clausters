The web port of the Python client's views/layers.py: the layer stack — every picture a signal view can draw, on one body. A view of a signal draws several things on one body, and layers is the list of them, back to front. It is one element and not a pile of widgets, because every view of a signal paints its own field before it draws: a second one on the same rectangle would be a lid over the first, not a layer over it. One element is also one time axis, one selection, one playhead and one upload. The panel on the right is the stack itself, a row per layer: a checkbox (is it drawn), a fader (its own weight over what is under it) and top (move it to the front). The order is yours, and it is live — top re-states the list with that layer last, and the picture re-composites between two frames. The vertical belongs to one layer, and here it is the top one: a spectrogram measures hertz and a wave measures amplitude, so whichever layer is on top takes y: "axis" and the rest take y: "box" — raise the wave over the texture and the left ruler goes from hertz to amplitude. That the top layer rules is this example's policy, not the host's: the host asks only that exactly one layer claim the axis, and refuses a stack where two claim it for different quantities. Alpha is per layer, and a hidden layer keeps its place — uncheck one and check it again. The two loudness curves never take the ruler: a loudness is read in LU against its own target, so that layer draws its own scale on the right of the body (see views/loudness.html). signal is the band-limited reconstruction and appears once you have zoomed into the samples. The take is four seconds of a sweep from 300 Hz to 3 kHz whose level steps down halfway, built at 16 kHz: the sweep is a diagonal in the texture and the step is a cliff in the wave. It makes no sound. Needs ./build.sh in clients/web first (dist/ is git-ignored); serve it over HTTP (python3 -m http.server) and open /examples/views/layers.html.
