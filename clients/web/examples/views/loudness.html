The web port of the Python client's views/loudness.py: loudness, drawn over the take it measures -- and over a bus, live. A peak says how close a signal came to full scale; it says nothing about how loud it sounds. Loudness does, and it is the number a delivery specification asks for: EBU R 128 wants a programme at -23 LUFS. measure: "peak momentary" puts the loudness curve over the samples -- what a meter would have read at each point of the take, over the 400 ms window up to it. The curve is read against the target line (-23 LUFS) and the LU ruler on the right of the body, because a loudness is not an amplitude and has no business on the amplitude axis the samples are drawn against. The numbers over the picture are the whole of R 128: the integrated loudness, the loudness range, the true peak and the ratio between the last two; sweep a selection over either half and they re-measure it, marked SEL. The same measure over a bus is the live meter's own curve, at no extra API. The take is two halves, three seconds each: a stereo 1 kHz tone at -30 dBFS then the same tone at -17 dBFS, built at 8 kHz (a take carries its own rate, and the example sends it over the wire every run). A stereo tone's peak level in dBFS is its loudness in LUFS -- that is the calibration BS.1770's -0.691 exists for -- so the curve sits on -30 and then on -17, and the integrated figure falls between them, nearer the loud half because the quiet one is under the relative gate. The live half makes sound. Needs ./build.sh in clients/web first (dist/ is git-ignored); serve it over HTTP (python3 -m http.server) and open /examples/views/loudness.html.
