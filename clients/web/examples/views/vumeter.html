The web port of the Python client's views/vumeter.py: a meter read the way a meter is read -- decibels, a held peak and a clip lamp that stays lit until it is clicked. Four rules make a bar a meter, and all four are the shared crate's: the peak is the block's (the engine walks every sample and publishes the peak, so a reader at a screen's rate still catches a transient), a peak is held (the hairline across the column), the scale is decibels down to a floor the reader states -- the first two meters here are the same two buses on the 60 dB strip a mix is read on and on the 144 dB range of a 24-bit render, while the third carries neither ladder nor numbers, which is what a strip of meters in a track header is -- and an over is latched. What counts as an over is a run of samples at full scale, which only something walking samples can see, so the clipCount UGen counts them onto a control bus and the widget differences it. The tone is struck every couple of seconds rather than swelled, because a level that rises slowly shows the scale and none of the ballistics. The hits are far enough apart that the whole ballistic plays out between them: the column starts down at once, the mark waits hold seconds and only then walks down at decay decibels a second. Every fourth hit goes past full scale. Click a meter to clear its lamps. It makes sound.
