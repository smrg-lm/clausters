One take, opened on its own: the editor's dedicated signal view. In the multitrack a take is a clip's body — drawn at a clip's size, next to everything else in the piece. This opens the same element by itself, at the size of a window: FormEditor.openSignal, the sibling of the dedicated piano roll. It is an editor of the take, not a picture of it. openSignal composes a SamplesEditor — the same one edit opens over a bare buffer — joined to this piece's editing context, so a stroke drawn here writes the engine's buffer and undoes in the piece's own order. Drawing is a gesture the widget has to be put into (examples/editors/composed.html shows the toggle); this page stays on the picture and the measures. The stack. layers: ["peak", "rms"] draws two measures of one take: what the signal reached (the min/max envelope) and what it held (the level inside it). They are measures of one view — one axis, one ruler, one selection, one playhead, one upload of the samples — and the toggle shows and hides the body over the peaks, which costs one message and does not move the view. The measure costs no second read: both pictures come off the same peak pyramid, so zooming cross-fades them together. A selection is of the element. Sweep a range and the editor keeps it in beats, naming the element it was swept on — the value an operation is handed, not screen state. And a generator has no samples until it is rendered, so asking one for a signal view is refused before any window exists.
