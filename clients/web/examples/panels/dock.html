A GUI host gives each window it opens a canvas of its own. Booted with a dock it draws them all in one, and every window is a pane of it, side by side: the panes share its width, and a pane opened later makes room for itself. A pane is a window in everything but its frame -- it keeps its def, its events and its picture -- so the code below is the one it would be without the dock, and a press reaches the pane it lands in.

Every pane has a tab. Drag a tab onto another pane -- into its middle to join its tabs, near one of its edges to stand beside it -- and drag the line between two panes to give one more room. The same moves are verbs the code calls, and the arrangement they leave is data it can read and put back.

Along the dock's top is a strip of pages, each an arrangement of its own: a window can name the page it opens on, the strip switches them, and a tab dropped on a page's tab moves its pane there. Switching a page frees nothing.
