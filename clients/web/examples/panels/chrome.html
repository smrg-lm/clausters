An application's face: every element of chrome, in one window. A work surface is the middle of a window and the rest of it is chrome: the menus that name what can be done, the tools that do it, the tabs and dividers that say how the room is shared, the dialog that asks before something is lost. This window holds one of each, laid out the way an application lays them out: a menu bar along the top (the window's own menu, with actions, a check, a group where one of several is on, a submenu and a disabled entry); a toolbar of flat buttons whose faces are glyphs, split into groups, with a spring and a button that opens a menu of its own; a split row whose divider you drag; a sidebar of sections that fold on their title strips; tabs over five pages -- one choice in each of its six views, the controls with their other looks, a plane with scroll bars, a pager, and rows of data: a table whose order is the page's and a tree; a context menu on the pages (the secondary button) and a tip on every tool; and a dialog, opened by Info > About and closed by freeing it, and a file chooser in another, opened by File > Open: the host lists the directory itself -- the page's own storage here, the disk in the script; and keys for the bar's verbs, shown beside its entries: F1 is About, Ctrl+O is Open, and M is a verb no menu names. It also shows what they share. Every control takes the focus: Tab walks them, Space or Enter presses, the arrows step. A press on a title of the bar opens its list and the arrows walk it; Escape closes it. And nothing here keeps state for the page: a folded section, a moved divider and a checked entry are props the host reports when a hand changes them -- the lines this prints.
