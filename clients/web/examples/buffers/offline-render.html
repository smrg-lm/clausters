The web port of the Python client's offline_render.py and plotting.py in one page: a piece rendered faster than real time in this tab, looked at, and downloaded — with no audio device and no server anywhere. The third carrier is not a carrier at all. Session.nrt() gives a Server whose connection writes time instead of waiting for it: every command a piece emits is accumulated as a timestamped score, and session.render() drains the clock — logically, with no sleeping — and hands that score to the engine's own renderer, the same wasm that makes this page's sound, running as fast as the machine manages. Above it sit the two verbs. render(x) is the change of state: a def, a bare expression, a pattern or a timeline goes in, and what comes back is samples plus what the take measured — frames, per-channel peak and RMS, and the seed, which is how you get a take back (hand it to the next render and it repeats sample for sample). plot(x) is its visual sibling: the same render, drawn in its own window rather than returned. Nothing here needs a gesture except the last button: an offline render has no AudioContext to unlock. That is the point of it — the piece is evaluated, not performed. What to look for: the noisy def rendered twice is two different takes, and rendered again with the first take's seed it is that take back, sample for sample. The seed governs the server's stochastic UGens; the amp jitter in the phrase is a Pwhite, which is the client's own seeded stream — two kinds of randomness, reproduced from two different places. Needs ./build.sh in clients/web first (dist/ is git-ignored); serve it over HTTP (python3 -m http.server) and open /examples/buffers/offline-render.html.
