The audio editor's lifecycle, walked: two windows, several files, one shared undo order, the clipboard between them, and saves that fail, that are edited after and that close the window. Each button below is a step, and each says what to look at and what to listen for; status prints what every window holds and activity what every window is costing. The order is the one to take them in, and every step leaves the windows ready for the next.
Two windows. first opens a.wav (mono, a decaying 440 Hz tone) and then b.wav (stereo, 660 Hz) and c.wav (six channels, 880 Hz); second opens d.wav (mono, 550 Hz). Each window keeps its own selection, cursor, transport and meter.
Playback across a change of focus. In first, put the cursor on a.wav, press L and Space: it loops. Click b.wav in the list on the left: the loop goes on, no play cursor is drawn over b.wav, and the meter shows two channels at zero. Click c.wav: six channels at zero. Click a.wav again: one channel, moving, and its play cursor is back. Space stops. second played meanwhile moves nothing in first.
Independent and shared history. a.wav and d.wav share one undo order (sharedHistory): cut second cuts d.wav, and undo first -- Ctrl+Z in first over a.wav -- puts it back without moving first's focus. b.wav and c.wav each have an order of their own: an edit there is undone only there.
The clipboard's lifetime, and a change of type. copy first copies a span of a.wav; paste second pastes it into d.wav (mono into mono). close origin closes a.wav in first, and paste second again still pastes: the copy outlives the file it came from. paste stereo pastes the mono copy into b.wav and is refused -- a paste never converts channels -- and b.wav is unchanged. Copy text in any other program and press Ctrl+V over a take: text stays text, and the paste is refused.
Saves. failed save saves d.wav into a directory that does not exist: the write is refused, the error is printed, and status shows the file still dirty and still called d.wav. save then edit saves it, then cuts it: dirty again, since the cut is newer than what was written; undo second brings back the written take, and it is clean. save and close saves d.wav once more and closes second: the window goes once the write is done, and the file holds the take.
A write in flight is too short for a hand to edit through, so an edit, a focus change or a close landing while the reply is still out is checked by the client suites (their shared fault trace), not here.
What it costs. Leave both windows alone for two seconds and press activity: a stopped window ticks for nothing. Play in one and press it again: that window ticks for its meter and its play cursor, and the other still for nothing.
