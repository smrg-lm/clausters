The demand family: a sequence that lives inside the def, pulled by the server rather than sent by the page. A demand UGen is a stream — it has no samples, only a next value, and it yields one each time a driver asks. duty(dur, reset, level) is such a driver with a clock of its own, and both of its inputs are streams: a dseq of durations against a dseq of pitches, the two free to be different lengths, which is what makes the pattern take longer to repeat than either of them. Nesting is the whole idea: an item of a dseq may itself be a stream, and then it is drained rather than taken once — the dgeom below contributes four rising pitches every time the sequence comes round to it. dxrand picks without ever repeating itself immediately, and dbrown walks the detune by small steps, folded into its bounds. This is the counterpart of the client-side sequencing in transport/sequencing.html, and the contrast is the point: there a routine on the page's clock sends a message per note; here nothing is sent after the synth starts. The page can stall and the sequence keeps its time. Everything sounds through the in-page engine: no server process, no socket. Swapping to a --ws server is the one line marked below.
