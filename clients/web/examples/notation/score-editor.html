The web port of the Python client's score_editor.py: editing a score by hand — the page, the model, and every verb between them. score.html plays an engraved phrase and drags a note; compose.html builds a piece by operating on it; this one opens a document somebody else typed and edits it the way a score editor does. A document becomes a model. The phrase below is ABC. Until it is read (score.sheet()) it is a picture: it draws, it plays, and not one verb applies to it. A page is a document, and a document was decided. The title, the double bar dividing the two halves and the system break are written into the model, because each is a statement somebody made. What the engraver decides when nobody decided is not stored and is not loss — it is worked out again identically every time. A gesture names a place; the model names the note. A click reports the element under the cursor and notation.itemId turns it into the model item it was written from — n7, n7-2 (a piece split across a barline) and n7-p1 (one pitch of a chord) are all item 7. From there every verb applies. Writing a note. With entry a press on blank paper reports where the press landed — a place, not a note — and insert puts one there, the pitch worked out from that staff's clef and the key. What is written is what is heard, because the piece is read out of the model: a staccato you just added shortens the sound and moves no attack. Click a note to select and hear it, drag one up or down the staff to move it, press blank paper inside the staff to write a quarter. The buttons act on the selection. Everything runs on the in-page engine: no server process, no socket.
